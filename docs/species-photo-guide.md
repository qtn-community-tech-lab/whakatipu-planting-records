# Species photo guide

The app’s **Species → Species reference guide** (`/species/photos`) covers all **95 species-register entries with planting-history rows**, ranked by recorded quantity across all sites and seasons. Quantities include records needing review. These entries total **140,068 recorded plants**; they are not a count of independently verified taxa or plants surviving today.

There are **94 sourced botanical profiles and one unverified-name review**, with **280 hosted image references**: 268 Wikimedia Commons and 12 iNaturalist. **93 entries have three photos, Veronica biggarii has one, and Cyathoides juniperina has none pending identification.** There are 277 unique image URLs: the three verified Veronica cupressoides synonym references appear under two separate WRT IDs. Profiles, photos and research outcomes are centralised by species ID and used on the main Species page, guide and species detail pages.

### How the additional 45 were checked

Three Sol subagents each researched three batches of five species, nine batches in total. For every selected image, they opened and viewed the actual image, checked the source’s taxon and attribution, recorded the licence, and compared visible features against botanical descriptions. Source descriptions, visual observations, synonyms, exclusions and search outcomes are retained in [the nine research batches](../data/research/species-research/). Automated checks compare these reviewed batches with the exact profiles and photos published in the central catalogue.

**A confirmed reference means the source supports that botanical species and the viewed image is consistent with it. It does not independently authenticate the source’s identification or confirm the nursery stock represented by a historical WRT record.** Photos from other locations cannot verify planting or survival at a WRT site. Similar species, subspecies and unresolved historical names still require identification of the actual plants, nursery records or a WRT ruling. A source taxon and a database name are linked only with documented identity or synonym evidence; unresolved IDs and quantities are never merged.

### Photo and identity gaps

- **Veronica biggarii (SP-154):** one suitable openly reusable photo confirmed. Other reviewed iNaturalist candidates were unlicensed, non-commercially licensed or unsuitable casual observations. Two more photographs remain needed; rarity does not justify substituting another species.
- **Cyathoides juniperina (SP-060):** the recorded name is unverified as written. No photos or identification traits are assigned. The verified Cyathodes/Leptecophylla relationship does not establish what this misspelled WRT entry intended.
- **Seven entries have three photos but retain historical identity questions:** Carex maorica, Leonohebe cupressoides, Olearia nummularifolia, Ozothamnus leptophyllus, Veronica hectorii, Carmichaelia crassicaulis and Leptecophylla juniperina. Their source taxon, spelling or subspecies limits are explained in each profile and review record.
- A Commons file called “Veronica hectori in Wellington Botanical Garden 04.jpg” was rejected because the visible broad leaves did not match the whipcord identification. Five Arthur’s Pass Veronica propinqua candidates were also excluded because their provenance did not support the checked native range; that is a suitability concern, not proof of misidentification.
- Museum herbarium images are explicitly labelled in the Austroderia richardii, Coprosma dumosa, Coprosma linariifolia and Carex virgata sets. Museum accession names can also accompany living-plant photographs; these are checked visually before captioning.

### Evidence and update procedure

- Ranking and quantities: [planting_history.csv](planning/prototype/planting_history.csv), grouped by species ID and sorted by descending total, then botanical name for ties.
- Register names and flags: [species_master_proposed.csv](planning/prototype/species_master_proposed.csv). Include entries with a history row even when quantity is zero.
- Shared published data: [species-profiles.json](../data/species-profiles.json), [species-photos.json](../data/species-photos.json) and [species-reference-reviews.json](../data/species-reference-reviews.json).
- Review evidence: [species-research](../data/research/species-research/) for ranks 51–95, plus the earlier source evidence linked below. Each new photo records its viewed image check, exact source, author, licence and date. Only short public observation references are retained; observation coordinates are not copied.
- Coverage and mapping checks: [species-profiles.test.ts](../tests/species-profiles.test.ts), [species-photos.test.ts](../tests/species-photos.test.ts) and [species-reference-research.test.ts](../tests/species-reference-research.test.ts). These verify all 95 history IDs, botanical-name matching, photo counts, distinct sources per ID and equality between new published data and reviewed batches. Automated tests validate the mapping; they do not identify a plant from pixels.
- After history, identity or reference changes, recompute this audit. Retain URLs and full licence/attribution metadata for future offline downloads. No photo binaries are committed, and offline display is not enabled.

## Species names and identification profiles

All 95 history entries have a reference record in `data/species-profiles.json` (94 botanical profiles and one unverified-name review), shown in the photo guide and on each species detail page. The main `/species` listing also displays the reviewed common and Māori names and a short description directly in each covered card. “Identification & sources” expands the identification features, name notes and source links without leaving the listing. Each includes a botanical name, common name, documented Māori names, a short description, identification features, source links and a check date. These are manually reviewed summaries, primarily from Wikipedia, with New Zealand botanical references for gaps. No Wiki MCP was available. Olearia lineata uses NZPCN and the Flora of New Zealand; Olearia fimbriata uses a DOC factsheet. WRT’s own planting guide supports the narrow-leaved tree daisy common name.

An unverified common name is stored as `null` and gets the same explicit fallback as Māori names; the profile title uses the botanical name. An empty Māori-name list displays “Not recorded in the sources checked.” It does not mean no Māori name exists. Shared names are explained, including kōwhai, toetoe and mingimingi/mikimiki. Carmichaelia petriei’s WRT name Mākaka is explicitly marked as a shared broom name without species-specific confirmation. Macrons are retained in display text. Profiles remain separate from WRT’s historical species register and do not change names, quantities or disputed taxa. The Olearia lineata and Olearia bullata profiles note NZPCN’s newer Shawia names while retaining the database names. Wikipedia’s mountain-flax article uses Phormium colensoi; the profile explains the alternative Phormium cookianum name without assigning an unrecorded subspecies. Poa cita’s possible southern name Pātītī is qualified as such, following Ngā Rauropi Whakaoranga. Hoheria glabrata has verified shared names Houhere, Houhi and Houi, and explains that the register’s Whauwhau was not confirmed in the checked references.

The ranks 36–50 extension adds verified Haumata, Tarata, Tūmatakuru, Putaputawētā and other names. Horoeka is explicitly shared by the two lancewoods; Akeake and Tauhinu are also qualified as shared names. NZPCN’s newer Shawia avicenniifolia and Shawia fragrantissima names are explained without changing WRT’s historical Olearia IDs. Veronica cupressoides has no suitable individual Wikipedia article, so its profile and photo species-reference link use NZPCN. The cypress-hebe photo set excludes the ornamental cultivar “Broughton Dome”.

Profile text is bundled with the app; it makes no live Wikipedia request. The source URLs remain available for review and future updates. Hosted reference images still need internet access. To extend coverage, review names and identification details against the species-specific sources, add a profile keyed to the correct species ID and run the coverage tests.

## Photo sources and reuse

`npm run import-workbook -- --write` imports profiles and photos into the species table, which supplies the app. The JSON files are import inputs only. `data/species-photos.json` holds the reviewed species mapping, caption, photographer, licence and licence link, Wikimedia Commons file page, species-reference article, optional observation URL, hosted image URL and source-check date. The browser loads the source-hosted thumbnails directly using `next/image` with `unoptimized`; the app does not download, cache or resize them on its server. No image binaries are committed. Image viewing needs internet access and depends on source availability; planting data stays local. Credits, licence links and source links appear with every photo.

Candidates came from Wikipedia’s lead images and Wikimedia Commons species categories, using the public [PageImages](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Extension:PageImages) and [Imageinfo](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/API:Imageinfo) APIs. There was no installed Wiki MCP. Candidate selection was checked against the Commons file descriptions rather than accepting the article’s lead image blindly:

- Olearia lineata and Olearia fimbriata have Commons photos even though their articles returned no lead image.
- Austroderia richardii uses the species-labelled Port Hills photo instead of the Milford Sound photo labelled only “Austroderia sp.”
- Corokia cotoneaster uses a photograph rather than the article’s botanical illustration.
- Podocarpus laetus retains the already-cropped Wiki source and mentions that in its caption.
- The ranks 36–50 source checks are retained in `data/research/photo-source-evidence/commons-top50.json`. Coprosma linariifolia has two living-plant photos and a labelled Auckland Museum herbarium specimen (AK362659).
- The ranks 21–35 source checks are retained in `data/research/photo-source-evidence/commons-top35.json`. Phormium cookianum images are catalogued under Phormium colensoi on Commons; Fuscospora fusca sources use Nothofagus fusca, which is stated in the photo captions. Coprosma dumosa has two living-plant photos and a labelled Auckland Museum herbarium specimen (AK360802).

These are illustrative reference photos taken at other locations. They are not evidence of a planting, its survival, a site identification or its current condition. The app uses rounded, cropped previews with an adjustable focal point to fit the Canopy theme. Full image links open the complete source-hosted reference. Thumbnail files were resized by their source; the app does not edit the downloaded assets. Each asset remains under its stated CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public-domain terms; see the linked source for full licence details. There is no blanket relicensing of these photos under an application licence.

The catalogue retains the image URLs, dimensions, species mapping and complete attribution so the same references can be downloaded for offline display later. Offline support is not enabled now. A later download process should verify that the source image and licence still match, save the files locally and retain the same credits. To extend coverage now, select a species-labelled reference, check its licence and attribution, add its hosted URL and metadata, and run the catalogue tests.

## Olearia hectorii fallback

Wiki’s species category (spelled “Olearia hectori”) and article provided only one suitable photo. With the user’s approval to use other openly licensed sources, two complementary photos of flower buds and bark were selected from John Barkla’s [research-grade iNaturalist observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/60402502). The API identifies the taxon as Olearia hectorii (404278) and the individual photos as CC BY. The official [iNaturalist licence module](https://github.com/inaturalist/inaturalist/blob/main/app/models/shared/license_module.rb) maps CC BY to version 4.0. Photo-level attribution and source evidence are saved in `data/research/photo-source-evidence/inaturalist-hectorii.json`; observation coordinates are not copied into the project. Source labels distinguish these photos from Wiki references.

## Browsing sublocations

Site pages link each entry in **Parts of this site** to the records page with both the parent site and exact parcel ID in its URL. The **Part of site** filter lets you switch between registered sublocations or return to all parts. Sorting, pagination and CSV downloads retain that selection; changing or removing the parent site clears it. A species summary shows the selected part’s recorded quantities, including unresolved records. Searching a site-group alias is not a reliable sublocation filter.

The species names link to their detail pages and reference photos where available. A future photo view for a selected sublocation can reuse this catalogue alongside any unresolved source/location notes. The separate wetlands walkthrough has been removed; its earlier version remains in the baseline commit.
